Common PGP Mistakes (and How to Avoid Them)

By OnlinePGP Editorial Team • Published November 23, 2026
Illustration: Flat illustration highlighting common cybersecurity and PGP mistakes

1. Confusing the Public Key and Private Key

The most dangerous mistake a beginner can make is sharing their private key instead of their public key. Remember: your public key is like an open padlock you hand out so others can lock messages for you. Your private key is the only key that opens that padlock. Never paste your private key into a public forum or send it to someone else.

2. Using a Weak or Reused Passphrase

A private key without a strong passphrase is like a locked safe with the key left in the door. Always protect your private key with a unique, high-entropy passphrase stored in a password manager.

Illustration: Common PGP Mistakes (and How to Avoid Them) diagram

3. Forgetting to Back Up Your Private Key

Because PGP is decentralized, there is no 'Forgot Password' button. If your laptop dies and you did not back up your private key, you will lose access to all past encrypted messages. Store an encrypted backup in at least two safe places.

4. Assuming Metadata and Subject Lines Are Encrypted

When you paste a PGP-encrypted block into an email, the message body is encrypted—but the To, From, Date, and Subject headers remain visible to your email provider. Never put sensitive information in the subject line.

5. Skipping Fingerprint Verification

Blindly trusting a public key you found online without verifying its fingerprint leaves you vulnerable to impersonation. Always verify fingerprints out-of-band, as explained in our fingerprint verification guide.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does PGP encrypt email subject lines?

Traditional PGP only encrypts the email body and attachments, not the subject line, sender, or recipient headers.

Can I recover a lost private key?

No. If you lose your private key and have no backup, any messages encrypted to it are permanently unreadable.

Is it safe to email my private key to myself?

No. Never send your private key over email or store it unencrypted in cloud storage.
