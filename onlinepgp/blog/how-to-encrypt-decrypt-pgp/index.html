How to Encrypt and Decrypt a Message with PGP Step by Step

By OnlinePGP Editorial Team • Published November 23, 2026
Illustration: Flat illustration showing step-by-step message encryption and decryption workflow

Before You Begin

To exchange encrypted messages with someone using PGP, you need two things: the recipient's public key (to encrypt messages to them) and your own private key plus passphrase (to decrypt messages they send to you).

If you don't have a key pair yet, generate a PGP key online first and share your public key with your contact.

Part 1: How to Encrypt a Message

1. Obtain the recipient's ASCII-armored public key. It starts with -----BEGIN PGP PUBLIC KEY BLOCK----- and ends with -----END PGP PUBLIC KEY BLOCK-----.

2. Open the Encrypt & Decrypt PGP Message section on OnlinePGP.com.

3. Paste the recipient's public key into the Enter the recipient's public key here box (or click Import Public Key to load a .txt file).

4. Type or paste your confidential message into the plaintext box and click ENCRYPT TEXT.

5. Copy the resulting -----BEGIN PGP MESSAGE----- block and send it to your recipient over email, chat, or any other channel. Even if intercepted, the ciphertext is unreadable without the recipient's private key.

Illustration: How to Encrypt and Decrypt a Message with PGP Step by Step diagram

Part 2: How to Decrypt a Message

1. Copy the entire encrypted message block, including the -----BEGIN PGP MESSAGE----- and -----END PGP MESSAGE----- lines. Missing even one dash will cause decryption to fail.

2. In the Decrypt section of our PGP tool, paste or import your private key.

3. Enter the passphrase that protects your private key.

4. Paste the encrypted message into the ciphertext box and click DECRYPT TEXT. Your original plaintext message will appear immediately.

Troubleshooting Common Decryption Errors

If decryption fails, check three things: first, confirm the sender encrypted the message using your current public key and not an old one. Second, verify that no extra line breaks or spaces were introduced by an email client. Third, double-check your passphrase for typos.

Frequently Asked Questions

Whose key do I use to encrypt a message?

Always use the recipient's public key—never your own public key (unless you are encrypting a note to yourself).

Why can't I decrypt a message I just encrypted?

Because it was encrypted with the recipient's public key, only the recipient's private key can decrypt it.

Can I encrypt attachments or files with PGP?

Yes, desktop tools like GnuPG can encrypt arbitrary binary files, while browser tools focus on text messages.
