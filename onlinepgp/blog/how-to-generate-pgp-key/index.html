How to Generate a PGP Key Safely

By OnlinePGP Editorial Team • Published November 23, 2026
Illustration: Flat illustration of a shield and cryptographic key pair generation in a browser

Why Key Generation Matters

Your PGP key pair is the foundation of your cryptographic identity. If your key is generated with weak randomness, protected by a guessable passphrase, or lost without a backup, your encrypted communications are at risk. Taking a few minutes to generate your key properly saves you from headaches later.

You can generate a PGP key online in seconds using our client-side tool, or use desktop software like GnuPG. In both cases, the same security principles apply.

Step 1: Choose Your User ID (Name and Email)

Every PGP key includes a User ID, typically formatted as Your Name <you@example.com>. Email clients like Thunderbird and Proton Mail use this email address to automatically match incoming public keys to your contacts.

If you are creating a key for work or everyday correspondence, use your real name and primary email address. If you are creating a key for anonymous or pseudonymous whistleblowing, use a dedicated pseudonym and avoid linking it to your personal accounts.

Illustration: How to Generate a PGP Key Safely diagram

Step 2: Pick a Strong, Unique Passphrase

Your private key is encrypted on disk (or in your download) using a passphrase. If someone steals your private key file, the passphrase is the last line of defense preventing them from reading your messages or impersonating you.

Avoid short passwords or dictionary words. Instead, use a passphrase of at least 4 to 6 random words (for example, generated via Diceware) or a 16+ character random string stored in a reputable password manager. Never reuse your email or banking password as your PGP passphrase.

Step 3: Generate and Download Both Keys

When you click Generate New PGP Keys Pair on OnlinePGP.com, OpenPGP.js uses your browser's cryptographically secure random number generator (window.crypto.getRandomValues) to create a 2048-bit RSA key pair locally.

Immediately download both your publicKey.txt and privateKey.txt files. Store your private key in an encrypted folder, a hardware-encrypted USB drive, or a secure password manager. Never upload your private key to cloud drives in plaintext or send it over unencrypted chat.

Step 4: Plan for Key Rotation and Revocation

Even well-protected keys should not live forever. Setting an expiration date (such as 1 to 2 years) on desktop GPG keys ensures that if you lose access to a key, contacts will eventually stop using it. If you ever suspect your private key was exposed, stop using it immediately and notify your contacts.

Frequently Asked Questions

What key size should I choose for PGP?

For RSA keys, 2048 bits is the minimum recommended size and 4096 bits is common for long-term keys. Modern Curve25519 (Ed25519/X25519) keys offer comparable security with much smaller key sizes.

What happens if I forget my PGP passphrase?

If you forget your passphrase, your private key cannot be unlocked and any messages encrypted to that key cannot be decrypted. There is no password reset in PGP.

Should I put my real name and email in my PGP key?

For standard email communication, yes, because mail clients match keys by email address. For pseudonymous use, you can use a pseudonym.
