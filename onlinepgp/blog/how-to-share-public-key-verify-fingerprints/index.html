How to Share Your Public Key and Verify Fingerprints

By OnlinePGP Editorial Team • Published November 23, 2026
Illustration: Flat illustration of two users verifying a cryptographic key fingerprint

Sharing Your Public Key Safely

Once you generate a PGP key online, the next step is making your public key available to the people who want to write to you. Common ways to share your public key include:

  • Attaching your publicKey.txt file to an email or hosting it at a URL on your personal website (for example, yourdomain.com/pgp.txt).
  • Publishing it on a modern keyserver such as keys.openpgp.org, which verifies your email address before indexing your key.
  • Adding your key fingerprint to your social media bio, GitHub profile, or business card.

Why Fingerprint Verification Is Critical

Imagine Alice wants to send an encrypted message to Bob. If an attacker intercepts Alice's search for Bob's key and substitutes a fake public key with Bob's name and email on it, Alice will unknowingly encrypt her secret message for the attacker.

To prevent this man-in-the-middle attack, Alice must verify Bob's 40-character key fingerprint through a separate, trusted channel—known as out-of-band verification. For example, Bob can read his fingerprint aloud over a voice call, send it via Signal, or print it on a physical card.

Illustration: How to Share Your Public Key and Verify Fingerprints diagram

How to Check a Fingerprint

In desktop tools like GnuPG, running gpg --fingerprint user@example.com displays the full 40-character hex string. Compare every character—not just the last 8 characters, which can be spoofed. Once verified, you can trust that key for future communications.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it safe to post my PGP public key online?

Yes. Your public key is designed to be shared openly. Just make sure you never post your private key.

What is a PGP fingerprint?

A fingerprint is a unique 40-character hexadecimal string derived from your public key, used to verify its authenticity.

Why do I need to verify a fingerprint?

Without fingerprint verification, an attacker could trick you into encrypting messages with their fake public key instead of your friend's real key.
