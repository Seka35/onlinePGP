Is Online PGP Safe? Client-Side Encryption Explained

By OnlinePGP Editorial Team • Published November 23, 2026

The Honest Answer: It Depends on Your Threat Model

Security professionals often debate whether web-based PGP tools are safe. The honest answer is nuanced: a transparent, 100% client-side tool like OnlinePGP.com is safe for everyday privacy, learning, and convenience—provided you understand how browser cryptography works and when to step up to offline tools.

What Client-Side Encryption Means Here

When you load OnlinePGP.com, your browser downloads static HTML and the open-source openpgp.min.js library. Once loaded, all key generation, encryption, decryption, and signing happen inside your browser's JavaScript engine. Your keys, passphrases, and messages are never transmitted to or stored on our server. You can inspect the full source code on GitHub.

How to Verify It Yourself (and Works Offline After Loading)

You do not need to take any website's word on faith. Open your browser's Developer Tools (F12), switch to the Network tab, and click GENERATE NEW PGP KEYS PAIR or ENCRYPT TEXT. Confirm that your key material and message text do not appear in any outgoing request payload. (Note: if you consented to analytics or ads, unrelated third-party requests may still appear in the log.)

In fact, once the page and /asset/openpgp.min.js have finished loading, you can disconnect from the internet and the cryptographic functions will continue to work locally (though ad/analytics scripts may log console errors while offline).

Real Risks of Any Browser-Based Cryptography

Even when a server never sees your keys, browser-based cryptography has real limitations you should understand: malicious or compromised browser extensions can read page inputs; a compromised operating system or shoulder surfer can capture what is on your screen; and any hosted JavaScript site relies on the integrity of the server delivering the script. Using a strong, unique passphrase mitigates key theft, but it cannot protect against a compromised device.

Cookies, Analytics, and Ads vs. Key Handling

OnlinePGP.com uses Google Analytics and Google AdSense (with Google Consent Mode v2) to measure traffic and fund hosting. Those cookies are completely separate from your local OpenPGP.js operations—your keys and messages are never sent to analytics or ad servers. Read our Privacy Policy for full details.

When to Use Offline GnuPG Instead

If you are a journalist, activist, or whistleblower facing targeted adversaries, or if you are managing high-value secrets and long-term master keys, use an offline, preferably air-gapped computer running GnuPG. For everyday learning and quick tasks, OnlinePGP.com offers a fast, accessible starting point. Read How to Generate a PGP Key Safely, Common PGP Mistakes, and What Is PGP?.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do my PGP keys leave my device on OnlinePGP.com?

No. All cryptographic operations run locally in your browser using OpenPGP.js. Your keys, passphrases, and messages are never sent to our server.

How can I verify a client-side PGP tool?

Open your browser's Developer Tools (Network tab) while generating a key or encrypting a message and confirm that no request payload contains your key or message data.

When should I use offline GnuPG instead?

For high-risk threat models, targeted attackers, long-term master keys, or life-critical secrets, use an offline, air-gapped computer running GnuPG.

Is a website with ads still safe for everyday PGP?

Yes, for everyday use: analytics and ad cookies are separate from local key processing, though you can always inspect the Network tab or use an offline tool for sensitive keys.
