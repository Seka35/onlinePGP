Is Online PGP Safe? Client-Side Encryption Explained

By OnlinePGP Editorial Team • Published November 23, 2026
Illustration: Flat illustration of a browser padlock showing local client-side cryptography

The Honest Answer: It Depends on Your Threat Model

Security professionals often debate whether web-based PGP tools are safe. The honest answer is nuanced: a well-built, 100% client-side tool like OnlinePGP.com is safe for everyday privacy, learning, and convenience—provided you understand how it works and when to step up to offline tools.

How Client-Side Encryption Works

When you load OnlinePGP.com, your browser downloads static HTML and the open-source openpgp.min.js library. Once loaded, all key generation, encryption, decryption, and signing happen inside your browser's JavaScript engine. You can even disconnect from the internet after loading the page and the tool will still work.

Illustration: Is Online PGP Safe? Client-Side Encryption Explained diagram

How to Verify It Yourself

Don't take any website's word on faith. Open your browser's Developer Tools (F12), switch to the Network tab, and click Generate New PGP Keys Pair. You will see that zero requests are made to any server during key generation, encryption, or decryption. You can also audit the full source code on GitHub.

When to Use Offline GnuPG Instead

Any website—even a static one—depends on the security of your browser, your operating system, and any browser extensions you have installed. If you are protecting life-critical secrets, managing high-value cryptocurrency keys, or operating under a targeted nation-state threat model, use an offline, air-gapped computer running GnuPG.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does OnlinePGP.com send my keys to a server?

No. All cryptographic operations run locally in your browser using OpenPGP.js. Your keys and messages never leave your device.

How can I verify that an online PGP tool is client-side?

You can open your browser's Developer Tools (Network tab) while generating or encrypting a key to confirm zero network requests are sent with your data.

When should I use offline GPG instead of an online tool?

For high-risk threat models, journalists in hostile environments, or long-term master keys, an offline, air-gapped GPG setup is recommended.
