PGP for Email: Thunderbird, Proton Mail, and Gmail

By OnlinePGP Editorial Team • Published November 23, 2026
Illustration: Flat illustration of encrypted email envelopes across desktop and webmail clients

Choosing the Right Email Setup

While OnlinePGP.com is ideal for quick, copy-and-paste encryption in any browser, people who send encrypted emails every day often prefer integrating PGP directly into their email client. Here is how the three most popular options work.

1. Mozilla Thunderbird (Built-In OpenPGP)

Thunderbird includes native OpenPGP support out of the box. You can generate a new key or import an existing private key under Account Settings → End-to-End Encryption. Once configured, Thunderbird automatically signs and encrypts messages whenever you have a recipient's public key.

Illustration: PGP for Email: Thunderbird, Proton Mail, and Gmail diagram

2. Proton Mail (Automatic OpenPGP)

Proton Mail handles OpenPGP encryption automatically between Proton users. For external contacts who use standard PGP, you can upload their public key in your contact settings and exchange standard PGP-encrypted emails seamlessly.

3. Gmail and Webmail

Standard Gmail does not support OpenPGP natively. To send PGP-encrypted messages from Gmail, you can either install an open-source browser extension like Mailvelope or encrypt your text locally using OnlinePGP.com and paste the resulting -----BEGIN PGP MESSAGE----- block into your Gmail compose window.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Gmail support PGP natively?

No. Standard Gmail does not have built-in OpenPGP support, so you need a browser extension like Mailvelope or a client-side tool like OnlinePGP.com.

Does Thunderbird support PGP?

Yes. Mozilla Thunderbird has built-in OpenPGP support without requiring third-party plugins.

Does Proton Mail use PGP?

Yes. Proton Mail uses OpenPGP under the hood and lets you import or export PGP keys.
