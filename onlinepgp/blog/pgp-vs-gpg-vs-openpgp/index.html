PGP vs GPG vs OpenPGP: What Is the Difference?

By OnlinePGP Editorial Team • Published November 23, 2026
Illustration: Flat illustration comparing PGP, OpenPGP standard, and GnuPG software

Why People Confuse PGP, GPG, and OpenPGP

If you have ever searched for email encryption, you have probably seen the terms PGP, GPG, and OpenPGP used interchangeably. While they are closely related, they refer to three distinct things: a commercial product, an open standard, and a free software implementation.

1. PGP (Pretty Good Privacy)

PGP was the original encryption program written by Phil Zimmermann in 1991. Over the years, PGP was commercialized and acquired by several companies, eventually becoming Symantec Encryption Desktop. Today, people often say 'PGP' casually to refer to any OpenPGP-compatible encryption.

Illustration: PGP vs GPG vs OpenPGP: What Is the Difference? diagram

2. OpenPGP (The Open Standard)

In 1997, the Internet Engineering Task Force (IETF) standardized the format of PGP keys, messages, and signatures into an open specification called OpenPGP (RFC 4880, and later RFC 9580). Because OpenPGP is an open standard, anyone can build compatible software without paying licensing fees. OnlinePGP.com uses OpenPGP.js, a JavaScript implementation of this standard.

3. GPG / GnuPG (GNU Privacy Guard)

GnuPG (commonly abbreviated as GPG) is a free, open-source command-line implementation of the OpenPGP standard maintained by the GNU Project. It is pre-installed on many Linux distributions and powers tools like Gpg4win on Windows and GPG Suite on macOS.

Which One Should You Choose?

Because they all share the OpenPGP standard, you do not have to worry about lock-in. Use OnlinePGP.com for quick, zero-install browser encryption, Thunderbird or Proton Mail for daily email, and GnuPG for automated scripts or offline key management. Read more in Is Online PGP Safe?.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are PGP and GPG compatible with each other?

Yes. Both follow the OpenPGP standard (RFC 4880 / RFC 9580), so keys and messages created in one work in the other.

Which one should I use?

For most users, GnuPG (GPG) on desktop, OpenPGP.js in the browser, or Thunderbird's built-in OpenPGP are the best choices.

Is OpenPGP a piece of software?

No. OpenPGP is a technical specification (standard), not a program you install.
